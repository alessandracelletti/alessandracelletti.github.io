The group

Mathematics and space

Space sciences require a broad and complementary range of mathematical expertise.

From the N-body problem to trajectory design, from the modelling of plasmas and planetary atmospheres to the interpretation of large cosmological datasets, SPACEMATH was created to foster interaction among researchers with different expertise and to build a nationwide interdisciplinary network.

Mission and objectives →
Objective

Integrating mathematical expertise for space sciences.

SPACEMATH aims to consolidate a national community capable of addressing scientific and technological problems ranging from the Solar System and interplanetary missions to cosmology.

Research

Four scientific directions

Examples of topics combining methods from pure and applied mathematics with central problems in space sciences.

Learn more →
1

The N-body problem

Periodic and quasi-periodic solutions, variational and perturbative methods, computer-assisted proofs and machine learning.

2

Low-energy trajectories

Invariant manifolds and optimal control for dynamically feasible and efficient transfer orbits.

3

Many-particle systems

Celestial mechanics, statistical mechanics and fluid dynamics for complex particle systems.

4

Cosmological and space data

Harmonic analysis, stochastic geometry, numerical analysis, optimization, wavelets, neural networks and generative AI.

Collaborations

From mathematical research to the space sector

Interaction with agencies, research centres and industry is an integral part of the SPACEMATH vision.

The group aims to connect real problems in space sciences with advanced mathematical tools, while supporting the training of PhD students and postdoctoral researchers and their integration into universities, research centres, space agencies and companies.
